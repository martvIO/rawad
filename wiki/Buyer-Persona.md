## Primary persona — "the organized bride" (decision-driver)

- **Who:** A bride (often) or groom, late 20s–30s, Arab/Israeli, in a town in the
  north/center (e.g. Nazareth, Umm al-Fahm, the Triangle, Galilee). Mobile-first,
  WhatsApp-native, **not** technical. Comfortable in Arabic; may switch to Hebrew.
- **Job to be done:** *"Make my wedding look high-status and effortless — and don't
  make me chase 400 relatives for RSVPs by hand."*
- **Context:** 200–600 guests, large extended families, strong norms around how an
  invitation is delivered (in person = respect). Time-poor, stressed in the months
  before the wedding.
- **Top objections:** (1) price vs. free digital tools; (2) "can my family just do
  the delivery?"; (3) "will it look good / dignified enough?"; (4) trust — *do I
  know anyone who used them?* (reputation-driven market → see social proof below).
- **Decision triggers:** seeing a real sample; a friend/relative who used it
  (referral); proof that delivery is tracked and dignified (uniformed, photographed).
- **Where they are reached:** WhatsApp, Instagram, word-of-mouth in the community —
  **not** search. This is why the funnel's chosen primary action is a WhatsApp
  message ([[Communication Settings]]), not self-serve signup.

## The economic buyer — "the father/family who pays"

- Frequently a **parent funds the wedding**, including invitations. They care about
  **family honor / how it looks to relatives** and **value for money**, less about
  app features.
- Implication for copy & sales script: lead with *dignity, reputation, and
  done-for-you reliability* (uniformed delivery, proof, nothing forgotten), then
  features. The person on the WhatsApp chat may be relaying to whoever pays.

## Secondary segment split (validate)

- **Traditional vs. modern couples** likely want different things: traditional →
  the **physical/handwritten** plan + delivery dignity; modern → the **digital**
  plan + photo-finder + design control. The landing page already presents both
  ([[Digital Invitations]]); copy could speak to each more explicitly once validated.

## How to validate (cheap, 1 week)
1. Pull your last ~10 paying customers: town, age band, plan (physical/digital),
   who decided, who paid, what they'd used otherwise.
2. Call 3–5: *"What made you choose us? What else did you consider? Who paid?"*
3. Add a one-line intake question for every new customer: **"How did you hear about
   us / what else did you consider?"** — builds a voice-of-customer loop over time.
